A visual essay and guide

What remains

Bitcoin’s unspent outputs, block by block

Every bitcoin in existence sits in an unspent output, and every one of those outputs is in this landscape. Seen from above it is a map: time runs left to right, from the first block in 2009 to the newest; amount runs upward, from dust to whole coins to thousands of bitcoin. Height and colour show how much remains in each place. Your browser replays the real chain to draw it.

I

I. Field

Seen from beyond its newest block, the landscape runs back to 2009 at the horizon. The near edge glows where the chain is still being written. Everything behind it is what remains: outputs that were created at some block and have not been spent since.

Explore this view ↗
Read the data

A cell covers 64 blocks of creation time and one of 2,072 amount rows. Height and colour show weighted density: an output of up to 5 BTC counts one; a larger output counts its amount divided by 5 BTC, so large holdings stay visible among the small. Height follows a logarithm of that value.

II

II. The map

From above, the field becomes a map. Time runs left to right, from the first block in January 2009 to the newest, at the right edge. Amount runs upward on a scale where each step is ten times the one below: single satoshis at the bottom, whole coins in the middle, 10,000 BTC and more at the top. Every point on the map is a cell, and bright lines are amounts that many outputs share.

V

V. Time

Now let eight days of real blocks run: 1,200 blocks, from 969,652 to 970,852, with the camera still. At block 969,912, seventeen of the cell’s outputs are spent in a single block. A spent output leaves the unspent set; its bitcoin is not destroyed but moves into new outputs at the edge. The column grows to 232 outputs while its last blocks are written, then only loses them, down to 94.

The cell holds 214 outputs at block 969,652, 160 at block 969,911, 143 after block 969,912, 108 at block 970,345 and 94 at block 970,852. Flashes mark spends, brighter for more bitcoin moved; spends of coins created within the last four weeks are dimmed, because young coins move constantly.

VI

VI. From above

The same eight days, from above. Each flash is a spend: outputs leaving one cell in that block, drawn where their coins were made, so the years along the bottom read as their age. Most flashes crowd the newest edge, where young coins move all the time. The rest are old coins waking up anywhere back to 2009: 1,000 BTC made in 2018, 100 BTC from 2019.

VII

VII. Readings

Colour can answer different questions. In both views height shows the number of outputs. In the first, the warmer the colour, the more outputs: the crowded rows of small amounts burn brightest. In the second, the warmer the colour, the more bitcoin: the crowded rows go dark, and the rows above 5 BTC light up.

Practical

The guide

Navigation

There are two ways to move. The app opens in map mode: drag to pan, and the point you grabbed stays under the cursor. Right-drag or Shift-drag orbits around the point under the cursor, the wheel or a trackpad pinch zooms toward it, and a double-click flies there. P opens Places, which jumps to the eras or flies to the amount bands, from the 50 BTC coinbase row to 1 to 100 sat.

In fly mode you steer the camera yourself. F, or the Fly button, switches to it: the mouse looks around, WASD flies, E and Q go up and down, Shift is 4x faster and the wheel sets the speed. Esc returns to map mode.

Touch

On a touch screen, map mode follows the conventions of map apps: every gesture keeps the ground under your fingers where it is. Pinch, twist and a two-finger drag combine in one movement.

Phones get their own layout. The timeline keeps the scrubber, transport, the speed menu, and Places, Settings and Help; in portrait the inspector and the settings open as sheets from the bottom. Fly mode needs a keyboard.

Time and playback

Space plays or pauses. The speeds are 0.5x, 1x, 2x, 5x and Max, on the keys 0 to 4 or the speed menu in the timeline; 1x is 60 blocks, about 10 hours of Bitcoin, every second. [ and ] step one block back or forward, exactly; with Shift they step 1,008 blocks, about a week. The timeline also accepts a block height or a UTC date.

N, or the Follow button, follows the tip: the camera moves with the newest block, while playing and when you scrub or step, and you can still pan, orbit and zoom. N again stops. Every block you land on, by seeking, stepping or playing at 1x, is the exact unspent set after that block. Online, 5x and Max follow the speed of your connection, and the HUD says so when playback is download-limited.

M, or the Sound button, turns on a click for each spend flash on screen while the chain plays, darker for older coins and louder for more bitcoin. Sound is off at first.

The inspector

Point at the landscape and a card under the block panel names the cell under the cursor: its range of amounts, its 64 blocks and the days they were mined, and the unspent outputs and value it holds at the current block. It reads data already in your browser, so it follows the cursor.

Click the cell, or press I with the cursor on it, to open the inspector. A ring marks the cell in the landscape. Below a heading with the cell’s column and row, its blocks and its exact range of amounts, the drawer has four parts. Here it is open on the column of about 1 BTC outputs that the essay follows.

At this block
Worked out in your browser by the same replay that draws the landscape: the unspent outputs and their value, the weighted density that sets height and colour, the bitcoin spent from the cell recently, and how the outputs split between up to 5 BTC and above.
History index
The cell’s complete record from the server: every output ever created in it, in which block, and when it was spent. A tick confirms that the record and the replay agree to the satoshi.
Lifecycle
How many outputs the cell has ever received, how many are alive at this block, how many are still unspent at the latest block the index covers, and the median lifespan of its outputs. The chart draws its unspent outputs over time.
Outputs
The outputs themselves: those still unspent, those spent after this block (earliest first) and, folded away, those spent before it, each with its amount, its creation block and its age.

Its last line opens the same place in the 2D explorer at bitcointimelapse.com, at the nearest pixel. On a phone, tap a cell and the inspector opens as a sheet.

Looks

A look sets the light, palette and measure in one choice; the data stays the same. Eleven are built in, each one click away in the settings, and Save… stores your own. The settings also change one thing at a time: the cells’ shape (smooth, columns, boxes or stepped), how tall the big coins stand, and the time of day.

Quality

Performance
75% resolution, FXAA, no shadows or ambient occlusion, and cylinders only close to the camera.
Balanced
Native resolution, SMAA, two shadow cascades, SSAO and bloom, with cylinders where cells are 6 px wide or more.
High
Three shadow cascades, SSAO with 16 samples, fog, sky and stars, and 12-sided cylinders where cells are 4 px wide or more.
Ultra
High plus 4096 shadow cascades, volumetric light and god rays: 60 fps in the overview at 4K on an M4 Max.
Extreme
Deliberately GPU-bound, for GPUs beyond an M4 Max: supersampling, SSR, finer detail and up to 2M cylinders.

A first visit starts at Balanced with automatic resolution scaling. A browser without WebGPU, a touch-only device or one that reports 4 GB of memory or less starts at Performance and says so. Choosing a quality turns the automatic scaling off. Ultra, Extreme and the cylinder columns need WebGPU; ?webgl=1 forces the WebGL2 fallback, capped at High.

Sharing

The address keeps the block, camera, navigation mode, look, quality and the settings that differ from them, so a copied link reproduces the view; Copy link in the settings does the same. A link without a block opens at least 1,008 blocks, about a week, before the last block, so Play has something to play.

Under the hood

  • Grid64 blocks per column and 2,072 amount rows. Every 64 new blocks add a column at the edge.
  • ReplayA Web Worker in your browser replays the change log from snapshots, so every block is exact.
  • DataThe dataset is read in pieces as you move and play, from the same server that answers the inspector’s history lookups.
